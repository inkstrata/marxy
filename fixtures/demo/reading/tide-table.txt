Tide table

Low water at six. The harbour
gives back what it borrowed:
a boot, a rope gone green,
the ribs of a boat no one claims.

We walk out on the sand
as if the sea had signed for it,
reading the ripples like a page
that someone left mid-sentence.

At noon the water turns.
It does not hurry. It does not
explain. It comes back in
the way a held breath does,

and by two the boot is gone,
the rope, the ribs, our footprints,
the whole of the morning's argument,
    folded under, kept, not lost.
