# The case for ragged lines

A ragged right edge is easier to read than a line forced to both margins. Justified text has to put the spare space somewhere, and a browser puts it between words, one line at a time, without looking ahead. The result is a column with loose lines and, where several loose lines stack up, rivers of white running down the page.

Book typesetters avoided this by looking at the whole paragraph before breaking any line, and by hyphenating. On a screen, where the measure changes with the window, ragged text with hyphenation gets most of the benefit and none of the rivers.

The reader does not notice good line breaks. They notice bad ones, and they blame themselves for losing their place.
