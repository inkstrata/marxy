# Prior art is thin, but its lessons are sharp

The Tauri ecosystem has no mainstream markdown-reader flagship to copy wholesale, but the prior art that does exist — a handful of small reader apps, two design-forward Tauri utilities, Tauri's own CI tooling, and typography research from apps that aren't Tauri at all — converges on specific, checkable lessons for Marxy. The clearest wins are process ones: **`Swatinem/rust-cache` plus `sccache` collapse an hour-long cold Tauri build** ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/tomtomdu73/ship-your-tauri-v2-app-like-a-pro-github-actions-and-release-automation-part-22-2ef7)), and Tauri's own repos decompose CI into narrow per-crate matrix jobs rather than one monolithic pipeline ([tauri-apps/tauri PR #874](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/pull/874)) — both are drop-in, no ADR needed. On the design side, the closest analogues — **markdown-viewer** and **md-reader**, both confirmed Tauri 2 reader apps — already ship outline scroll-sync, keyboard-consistent navigation, `prefers-reduced-motion` support, and instant local-only theme switching ([markdown-viewer](https://github.com/putuandy/markdown-viewer), [md-reader](https://github.com/olafkrawczyk/md-reader)), which reads as a checklist Marxy should already clear given its own bar ("a competent, ordinary-looking markdown viewer is a failure"). But several headline research findings — a duospaced font, real font-metrics baseline alignment, a fully-collapsing titlebar — are aesthetic or contract-level changes that Marxy's own rules require routing through an ADR or a taste-review queue row, not landing as quiet drive-by edits. Just as important is what to *un-learn*: several apps repeatedly cited as Tauri prior art (Warp, RustDesk, Obsidian, AFFiNE) are verified **not** to be Tauri, or to have left Tauri, and must not be cited as precedent anywhere downstream.

## Verified case studies are a short, specific list — and several famous "Tauri apps" are false leads

Tauri maintains no official showcase; the working references are `awesome-tauri` and madewithtauri.com, and even those need per-app verification against the app's own `tauri.conf.json`/`Cargo.toml` rather than trust in an aggregator listing ([GitHub Discussion #7643](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/discussions/7643)). That verification effort mattered: **Warp is not Tauri** — its own engineering blog says the team briefly tried Electron, then built a custom Rust/Metal-GPU UI framework called "warpui," and a low-quality secondary source (lushbinary.com) falsely claims otherwise ([Warp: How Warp Works](https://www.warp.dev/blog/how-warp-works)). **RustDesk is not Tauri** — its UI is Flutter (with a legacy Sciter fallback for 32-bit Windows only), and the team explicitly debated and rejected Tauri in favor of Flutter in their own GitHub discussion ([RustDesk Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RustDesk); [GitHub Discussion #533](https://github.com/rustdesk/rustdesk/discussions/533)). **Obsidian is not Tauri** — it is Electron, confirmed across multiple independent sources ([InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/3547072/electron-vs-tauri-which-cross-platform-framework-is-for-you.html)). **AFFiNE has migrated away from Tauri to Electron**, per its own community forum thread "Why Shift from Tauri to Electron" ([AFFiNE Community](https://community.affine.pro/c/questions-answers/why-shift-from-tauri-to-electron)) — though the notes flag this as based on a thread title/snippet only, not a fully-read primary source, so treat the AFFiNE finding itself as strongly indicative rather than fully confirmed. And "Tabby" is two unrelated projects: the well-known terminal (`Eugeny/tabby`) is Electron, while a much smaller, newer, unrelated project sharing the name is Tauri ([Eugeny/tabby](https://github.com/eugeny/tabby); [markbrutx/Tabby](https://github.com/markbrutx/Tabby)). Any future research or design document that cites Warp, RustDesk, Obsidian, or AFFiNE as Tauri precedent is repeating a debunked claim and should be corrected on sight.

What survives verification is a short, genuinely useful list. **Spacedrive** (Tauri 2 + React 19 + TanStack Query, a Rust-backed file manager) and **Aptakube** (Tauri + Solid.js, a Kubernetes GUI explicitly marketed against Electron competitors) demonstrate that a Tauri app can look and feel premium outside the reader category — both worth studying as chrome/palette/settings-polish references precisely because they aren't readers and so prove the ceiling is achievable generally, not just as a lucky fit for note apps ([Spacedrive GitHub](https://github.com/spacedriveapp/spacedrive); [Aptakube](https://aptakube.com/)). Within the reader niche itself, **md-reader** (Rust core + React/CodeMirror, "local-first, zero telemetry") and **markdown-viewer** (Rust + Svelte 5) are the two closest direct analogues to Marxy — same category, same no-account/local-first posture, and both confirmed via their own READMEs to be Tauri 2 ([md-reader](https://github.com/olafkrawczyk/md-reader); [markdown-viewer](https://github.com/putuandy/markdown-viewer)). `oratis/Markup` — "read your Markdown like a web page, edit on demand" — is the single closest positioning match to Marxy's "reader first, editing is transformation" philosophy found anywhere in this research, though its design rationale wasn't documented deeply enough to extract further findings from it ([oratis/Markup](https://github.com/oratis/Markup)).

## Concrete UI patterns to adopt, adapt, or explicitly reject

Four UI questions had answers with enough evidence to act on. First, **chrome**: Tauri's baseline mechanism is `decorations: false` plus `data-tauri-drag-region` for a fully custom titlebar, or macOS-only `titleBarStyle: Transparent` to keep native traffic lights while drawing custom content around them ([Tauri Window Customization docs](https://v2.tauri.app/learn/window-customization/)). Community plugins `tauri-plugin-decorum` and `tauri-plugin-frame` exist specifically because raw Tauri leaves gaps — traffic-light inset alignment and Windows 11 Snap Layout support — and are worth adopting directly rather than re-solving ([tauri-plugin-decorum](https://github.com/clearlysid/tauri-plugin-decorum); [tauri-plugin-frame](https://github.com/clarifei/tauri-plugin-frame)). But **no source anywhere in this research documents an app that collapses its titlebar to true zero height at rest and reveals it on hover/hotkey** — every existing plugin restyles persistent chrome, it doesn't make it vanish. Marxy's "chrome at rest is zero" ambition for the titlebar itself is therefore not derivative of prior art; it's custom work layered on `decorations:false`, and because it's chrome the reader sees, it needs a `docs/taste-review/queue.md` row, not just an engineering ticket.

Second, **focus/zen mode**: every verified reader-adjacent Tauri app that has one (Paperling, markdown_tauri, CrabPad, md-reader) implements it as a **named, keyboard-toggled state** — a hotkey or button that hides sidebar/toolbar — not a hover-proximity reveal ([CrabPad writeup](https://dev.to/ukash/i-built-a-markdown-editor-with-tauri-heres-what-i-learned-4f5l); [md-reader](https://github.com/olafkrawczyk/md-reader)). If Marxy's chrome-summon interaction relies on hover-edge detection rather than explicit toggling, that is a genuine design departure from everything documented in this space and should be validated in taste review rather than assumed to have precedent. One small, consistent, and cheap-to-adopt pattern across CrabPad and markdown-viewer: **persist the last-used mode/density across sessions** — reopening the app shouldn't silently reset zen mode or reader settings ([CrabPad](https://dev.to/ukash/i-built-a-markdown-editor-with-tauri-heres-what-i-learned-4f5l); [markdown-viewer](https://github.com/putuandy/markdown-viewer)). That's a drop-in engineering task, not a taste or contract question, provided the persisted values stay local (matching Marxy's no-accounts stance, which markdown-viewer also follows by storing theme/width/text-size only in webview local storage).

Third, **theming**: the ecosystem has converged on exactly what Marxy already does — semantic CSS custom-property tokens redefined per theme via a class or media query, with theme-switching delegated to Tauri's own native OS-theme API rather than the now-deprecated `tauri-plugin-theme` ([tauri-plugin-theme, deprecated](https://github.com/wyhaya/tauri-plugin-theme)). This is a validation, not a change: Marxy's `--marxy-*` contract is not an unusual choice, it's the pattern other Tauri apps landed on too. Nobody surveyed, however, ships a real third-party theme marketplace or user-authored-theme import flow — if Marxy wants that, it is ahead of documented prior art, with no pattern to borrow, and any such feature would itself need a contract-level ADR given that `packages/theme`'s `--marxy-*` contract is explicitly frozen.

Fourth, **command palette**: only one verified Tauri app (Spacedrive) documents a Cmd/Ctrl+K palette, and even its exact behavior (fuzzy vs. exact search, grouping) couldn't be confirmed from primary sources — the palette's guide page 404'd during research ([Spacedrive — Made with Tauri](https://madewithtauri.com/submissions/spacedrive)). No verified Tauri app was found using `cmdk` or `kbar` by name; the pairing of Marxy's outline-driven palette with `kbar`'s actions-registry model (nested commands, virtualization, shortcuts bound to actions) is ecosystem-plausible reasoning, not confirmed prior art, and should be treated as a build decision Marxy makes on its own merits rather than one backed by a named precedent.

## CI and release engineering: the fastest, lowest-risk wins in this research

The engineering-process notes are the strongest section of this research because they document official Tauri-repo behavior directly, not aggregator claims. Three findings are close to drop-in: caching (`Swatinem/rust-cache` or `mozilla-actions/sccache-action`, the latter needing only two env vars, `RUSTC_WRAPPER=sccache` and `SCCACHE_GHA_ENABLED=true`) turns Tauri's roughly one-hour uncached cold build into a tractable CI cost ([Swatinem/rust-cache](https://github.com/Swatinem/rust-cache); [Depot.dev](https://depot.dev/blog/sccache-in-github-actions)); passing `--locked` to `cargo build`/`test`/`check` prevents silent `Cargo.lock` drift and guarantees CI tests the exact locked graph ([corrode.dev](https://corrode.dev/blog/tips-for-faster-ci-builds/)); and splitting one monolithic clippy/test job into per-crate, per-feature matrix jobs (as `tauri-apps/plugins-workspace` and the main `tauri-apps/tauri` repo both do) pinpoints failures faster and lets unrelated crates report green independently ([plugins-workspace lint-rust.yml](https://github.com/tauri-apps/plugins-workspace/blob/v2/.github/workflows/lint-rust.yml); [tauri-apps/tauri PR #874](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/pull/874)). None of these touch `packages/*/src/contracts/`, none change what the reader sees, and none require an ADR — they're pure `scripts/`/CI-workflow changes that `pnpm precheck`'s gate structure can absorb directly.

Two findings validate rather than change existing Marxy stances. Bundle-size tooling in this ecosystem (`orf/cargo-bloat-action`) is purely advisory — it comments on PRs but never fails a build, and a parallel project attempting a hard gate is explicitly marked "not yet working" — which is consistent with, and arguably vindicates, Marxy's ADR-0032 stance of measuring performance/bundle-size without gating on it ([orf/cargo-bloat-action](https://github.com/orf/cargo-bloat-action); [Reloaded-Project WIP](https://github.com/Reloaded-Project/devops-cargo-bloat-action)). Similarly, no Tauri OSS project was found hard-failing CI on a bundle-size or startup-time regression at all — the research explicitly cautions that absence of evidence isn't proof of absence here, but it's the best evidence available. On visual regression, the maintained path is WebdriverIO's `@wdio/tauri-service`, which defaults to an embedded WebDriver running *inside* the app binary — the only way to cover macOS, since WKWebView has no first-party WebDriver tool at all ([Tauri WebDriver docs](https://v2.tauri.app/develop/tests/webdriver/); [WebdriverIO Tauri Service docs](https://webdriver.io/docs/wdio-tauri-service/)). The concrete warning for Marxy: testing a Tauri frontend against Playwright's bundled Chromium tests a *fourth*, non-representative rendering engine, since Tauri actually ships WebView2/Chromium on Windows, WebKitGTK on Linux, and WKWebView on macOS, and CSS that works on one can visibly break on another ([DEV Community: Cross-Platform Desktop Wars](https://dev.to/nikolas_dimitroulakis_d23/cross-platform-desktop-wars-electron-vs-tauri-how-do-you-explain-the-tradeoffs-to-users-2948)) — this validates that Marxy's existing per-engine screenshot baselines are the correct architecture, not a shortcut worth abandoning for a single shared baseline. Note this specific Playwright-mismatch claim traces to an independent personal-project wiki, not an official source, so treat it as directionally right rather than authoritative.

On code-signing, the best-documented pitfalls are notarization hangs (one case documented "stuck at notarizing... for more than an hour," [tauri-apps/tauri Discussion #8630](https://github.com/orgs/tauri-apps/discussions/8630)) and the fact that a free Apple Developer account cannot notarize at all. Several of the sharper claims here — certificate-expiry causing *silent* CI breakage, entitlements misconfiguration crashing a signed build that works fine unsigned — trace to secondary blog synthesis rather than a primary Apple/Tauri doc fetched directly in this research pass, so they're flagged as moderate-confidence pending a direct read of `v2.tauri.app/distribute/sign/macos/`. The actionable takeaway regardless: macOS signing/notarization should be its own explicitly-timed CI step with failure alerting, not folded silently into a generic build job where a hang or fallback-to-unsigned could go unnoticed. Separately, the contributor-workflow question (branching, PR templates, changelog automation at other small Tauri projects) came back as the weakest-sourced section of the entire research pass — no verified `CONTRIBUTING.md` or PR template was found for Spacedrive, RustDesk, Pake, or Tabby, so there is nothing concrete to adopt there beyond confirming Marxy's existing Conventional-Commits-plus-Jira-key convention is directionally aligned with what Tauri's own release tooling expects from commit history.

## Typography prior art comes from non-Tauri apps and points toward font-level, not layout-level, upgrades

None of the desktop apps famous for reading typography — iA Writer, Bear, Ulysses, Typora — are Tauri; they're native (Cocoa/AppKit, UIKit) or, in Typora's case, migrated to native WKWebView on macOS while staying Electron on Windows/Linux ([Typora GitHub issue #3960](https://github.com/typora/typora-issues/issues/3960)). Crucially, **none of them publish algorithm-level detail on line-breaking, hyphenation, or baseline-grid engineering** — their public engineering content is almost entirely about font design, not layout internals. What they do document, though, is directly transferable: iA Writer's **Duo** font gives wide glyphs (`w`, `W`, `m`, `M`) roughly 50% extra advance width, keeping monospace scanning quality without squished letterforms, and it's freely available on GitHub ([iA: From Monospace to Duospace](https://ia.net/topics/in-search-of-the-perfect-writing-font); [iaolo/iA-Fonts](https://github.com/iaolo/iA-Fonts)); Bear commissioned a custom typeface, Bear Sans, tuned specifically for on-screen reading rather than adapted from print ([Bear blog](https://blog.bear.app/2023/08/learn-about-our-new-custom-font-bear-sans/)). Evaluating a duospaced or purpose-tuned reading face for Marxy's default theme is a live option worth taking seriously — but because the default theme's specific values are taste, not mechanism, adopting one requires a story with a `docs/taste-review/queue.md` row, per Marxy's own contract, not a silent font swap.

On line-breaking libraries, there's a well-documented lineage — `bramstein/typeset` → `tex-linebreak` → `tex-linebreak2` → the newer WASM/HarfBuzz `knuth-plass-wrap` — and every one of them explicitly treats **measurement**, not the dynamic-programming algorithm itself, as the hard, pluggable part; the author of `bramstein/typeset` calls it "a proof of concept... not ready for production use" ([bramstein/typeset](https://github.com/bramstein/typeset/)), and `tex-linebreak`'s maintainers warn it's not float-aware and that DOM justification "can be slow in large documents" ([robertknight/tex-linebreak](https://github.com/robertknight/tex-linebreak)). None of them implement hanging punctuation or baseline-grid enforcement at all — that space is genuinely underserved in open source. The one implementation worth studying line-by-line is **OpticalMargin**, a cross-browser hanging-punctuation library that measures each glyph's actual visual overhang via Canvas `TextMetrics` rather than a lookup table, with default hang fractions of 1.0 for hyphens, 0.8 for quotes, and 0.6 for commas, clamped by a `threshold` and `maxHangRatio` ([over-punch/OpticalMargin](https://github.com/over-punch/OpticalMargin)) — these numbers are a genuinely useful sanity check for Marxy's own hang-amount table, since the CSS `hanging-punctuation` property itself is Safari-only and unusable cross-engine ([Chris Coyier](https://chriscoyier.net/2023/11/27/the-hanging-punctuation-property-in-css/)).

For font loading, the standard toolkit — `document.fonts.ready`/`FontFace.status` to gate any measurement pass until fonts are truly parsed, combined with `@font-face` fallback-metric overrides (`size-adjust`, `ascent-override`, `descent-override`) to prevent layout shift on swap — is directly applicable and mechanical enough to be a drop-in engineering task, especially since Marxy bundles its own theme fonts and so faces a narrower version of the problem than a typical website ([MDN: CSS Font Loading API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CSS_Font_Loading_API); [Simon Hearne](https://simonhearne.com/2021/layout-shifts-webfonts/)). More significant is the baseline-grid finding: the public web-dev consensus is that `line-height`-multiple heuristics are the practical ceiling because most implementations lack real glyph metrics — but Marxy's architecture, which already does per-glyph measurement for hanging punctuation and Knuth-Plass, is structurally positioned to do *true* baseline alignment via actual font ascent/descent data (fontkit- or opentype.js-style shaping output) rather than approximate it ([Smashing Magazine: CSS Baseline](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2012/12/css-baseline-the-good-the-bad-and-the-ugly/); [fontkit npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/fontkit)). No JS/TS library doing exactly this was found anywhere in the research — if Marxy's `packages/typeset` already enforces baseline grids from real glyph metrics, it is close to best-in-class for this niche, not derivative of prior art, and worth documenting as such rather than searching further for a precedent that doesn't appear to exist.

## Flagged for an ADR or a taste-review row — do not drop these in quietly

Per Marxy's own contract (`packages/*/src/contracts/` is frozen and needs an ADR to change; anything the reader visually sees needs a `docs/taste-review/queue.md` row), several findings above cannot be treated as simple engineering pull requests:

- **A fully-collapsing titlebar at rest** (true zero-chrome, not just restyled chrome) has no documented precedent anywhere in this research and is new interaction design — taste-review row required, and likely worth a design note since it also affects the window-chrome contract that `apps/desktop/src/shell` implements.
- **Hover-edge/proximity chrome summon** (as opposed to keyboard-toggled modes, which is what every verified precedent uses) is a genuine departure from documented practice — taste-review row required before treating it as validated by prior art, since it currently isn't.
- **Adopting a duospaced or custom reading typeface** for the default theme is a taste decision on frozen-contract-adjacent territory (the default theme's values, not the `--marxy-*` token contract itself) — needs a story with a taste-review row, per the existing rule that "the default theme's values are taste and need a story with a taste-review queue row."
- **A third-party/community theme marketplace or user-authored-theme import flow** — no prior art exists for this in the Tauri ecosystem, and because it would extend what the `--marxy-*` contract needs to support (e.g., validation, import scope), it plausibly requires an ADR before implementation, not just a feature branch.
- **True baseline-grid enforcement from real glyph metrics**, if not already fully implemented in `packages/typeset`, touches the frozen `ast.ts`/typeset contracts only if it requires new provenance or metric fields; if it's purely internal to `packages/typeset`'s rendering logic it may not need an ADR, but any new cross-package data (e.g., glyph metrics surfaced through the AST) would.

Everything else in this report — CI caching, matrix-job restructuring, `--locked` builds, per-OS visual-regression architecture, font-loading-shift mitigation, persisting last-used reader mode locally, and CSS-custom-property theming (already validated as-is) — is ordinary engineering work inside Marxy's existing gates and does not need either mechanism.

## What this changes about Marxy's direction

The most important negative finding may be the most useful: this research confirms that Marxy is not late to a category with an established design language to imitate. The Tauri markdown-reader niche is populated by small, single-maintainer projects, none with the track record of Typora or Marked 2, and even the two closest analogues (md-reader, markdown-viewer) are feature-comparable checklists rather than aesthetic benchmarks. That reframes Marxy's actual competitive reference set as native, non-Tauri apps (iA Writer, Bear) for typography and design-forward Tauri apps outside the category (Spacedrive, Aptakube) for chrome and polish — and it means several of Marxy's most ambitious claims (zero-chrome-at-rest, real-glyph-metric baseline grids) are not catching up to prior art but potentially setting it, which raises rather than lowers the bar for the taste-review process to get them right before they ship.
