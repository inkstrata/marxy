# A Dream of John Ball

Forsooth, brothers, fellowship is heaven, and lack of fellowship is hell: fellowship is life, and lack of fellowship is death: and the deeds that ye do upon the earth, it is for fellowship's sake that ye do them, and the life that is in it, that shall live on and on for ever, and each one of you part of it, while many a man's life upon the earth from the earth shall wane.

⋮

But while I pondered all these things, and how men fight and lose the battle, and the thing that they fought for comes about in spite of their defeat, and when it comes turns out not to be what they meant, and other men have to fight for what they meant under another name—while I pondered all this, John Ball began to speak again in the same soft and dear voice with which he had left off.

---

William Morris (1834–1896), from *A Dream of John Ball*, serialised in *Commonweal* in 1886–87 and published as a book by Reeves and Turner (London, 1888). The first passage is the priest John Ball, preaching at the cross in 1381; the second is the dreamer, who has come from the nineteenth century to hear him.
