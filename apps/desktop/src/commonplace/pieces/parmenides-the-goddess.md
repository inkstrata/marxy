# The Way of Truth

## Ἑλληνικά

χρεὼ δέ σε πάντα πυθέσθαι\
ἠμὲν Ἀληθείης εὐκυκλέος ἀτρεμὲς ἦτορ\
ἠδὲ βροτῶν δόξας, ταῖς οὐκ ἔνι πίστις ἀληθής.

## English

Welcome, O youth, that comest to my abode on the car that bears thee tended by immortal charioteers! It is no ill chance, but right and justice that has sent thee forth to travel on this way. Far, indeed, does it lie from the beaten track of men! Meet it is that thou shouldst learn all things, as well the unshaken heart of well-rounded truth, as the opinions of mortals in which is no true belief at all.

---

Parmenides of Elea (early fifth century BCE). The goddess greets the young traveller at the gates of Night and Day, in the proem of his poem *On Nature*. The Greek is lines 28–30 of fragment 1 in Hermann Diels's *Parmenides Lehrgedicht* (Berlin, 1897); Diogenes Laertius quotes the same lines. The English is John Burnet's, from *Early Greek Philosophy* (2nd ed., London: A. and C. Black, 1908), lines 24–30, without his line numbers.

Ἀλήθεια, *alētheia*: truth, and, in the word's own parts, what is no longer hidden.
