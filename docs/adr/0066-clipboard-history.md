# ADR-0066 — Clipboard history: Marxy keeps its own copies, and watches nothing

- **Status:** proposed (J-D1, 2026-10-10). The author rules; nothing is built from this record until then.
- **Date:** 2026-10-10
- **Reads:** commitment 2 (private by default) and commitment 3 (faithful to the bytes; Marxy's state in
  plain files) in `AGENTS.md`; ADR-0044 (nothing leaves without a reader action); ADR-0065 item 3
  ("Nothing watches the pasteboard"); ADR-0063 (the pattern for a privacy line).
- **Amends:** `docs/design/11-config-and-storage.md`, "What is never stored" (document contents), for the
  one case the reader chooses: `clipboard_history = "keep"`. Option B, if chosen instead, also amends
  ADR-0065 item 3.
- **Evidence:** [`docs/plan/direction-2026-10/studio/`](../plan/direction-2026-10/studio/README.md) (the
  design), [`history.md`](../plan/direction-2026-10/studio/history.md) and
  [`rules.md`](../plan/direction-2026-10/studio/rules.md); the mockup's "History policy and privacy"
  ([`mock-v2/06-clipboard.md`](../plan/direction-2026-10/mock-v2/06-clipboard.md)), which this record
  departs from.

## Context

A reader copies a command out of a log, copies a paragraph as Slack, copies a table as CSV, then copies
something else, and the first copy is gone. ADR-0065 made every copy good and left history to this
record, because a history is a store of what the reader copied: often document text, sometimes a
secret pasted into a `.env` file Marxy happened to show.

The mockup records every app's copies. It polls `NSPasteboard.changeCount` twice a second, keeps 500
items for 30 days in SQLite, and skips concealed, transient and auto-generated items. Three things in
that design break a rule already held: ADR-0065 item 3 says nothing watches the pasteboard;
commitment 3 says Marxy's state is plain files, never SQLite; and macOS 15.4 and later alert when an
app reads another app's copy without the reader's action, so a watcher either prompts on every copy
or reads nothing.

## Options

### A. Marxy's own copies, recorded where Marxy writes (no watching)

Marxy already knows every copy it makes: J-02's write capability is the one path a copy takes. History
records at that path, after the write succeeds. It needs no pasteboard read, no permission, no
background thread and nothing platform-specific, so it works on Linux too.

- **Recorded:** every non-transient write Marxy makes (`⌘C`, Copy as, Extract, a transform's result,
  a copy from history), with the representations written, the format, and the source: the document's
  path, byte range and line. Also what the reader adds with **Keep the clipboard**, which reads the
  clipboard once on that action, through J-02, types first, concealed items refused (ADR-0065 item 3
  already allows a read on a reader action).
- **Never recorded:** Marxy's own temporary writes (marked `org.nspasteboard.TransientType`);
  text that looks like a secret (below), unless the reader turns the check off; an item larger than
  1 MB; anything while history is paused or off. What was not recorded leaves one row in memory
  (source path, reason, time, never the text), never on disk.
- **Cost:** small. A pure model in core and a store in the desktop app (cards J-07, J-08).
- **Loses:** copies made in other apps. A reader who copies in Safari and wants it back later finds
  nothing, unless they pressed *Keep the clipboard*.

### B. Every app's copies, by opt-in watching, off by default

A second switch, `clipboard_watch = false` by default, which only a reader turns on, and only while
history is on.

- **How it watches:** a native thread reads `changeCount` (an integer; no content) every 500 ms
  while Marxy runs, never while it is quit. When the count moves it reads the item's `types` before
  any data. It records the source from `org.nspasteboard.source`, or the frontmost application at that
  moment.
- **Never recorded:** an item whose types include `org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType`,
  `org.nspasteboard.TransientType` or `org.nspasteboard.AutoGeneratedType` (its data is never read);
  an item from an excluded app (by bundle id; the default list is 1Password, Bitwarden, Keychain
  Access and Passwords, and the reader edits it); text that looks like a secret; an item over 1 MB;
  images and file references (until a later design); anything while paused. A *not recorded* row
  keeps the app, the reason and the time, never content, in memory only.
- **macOS:** on 15.4 and later, reading another app's copy without a reader action may show the
  system's paste alert, or fail when the reader set *Paste from other apps* to deny. Marxy then stops
  watching, turns the switch off and says so: "macOS is not letting Marxy read what other apps copy.
  History keeps copies made in Marxy." Behaviour on macOS 26 is checked on a current build by the
  story that builds it.
- **Cost:** large. A native watcher (amending J-01's "do not poll `changeCount`"), an exclusion
  list, the permission path, battery and wake-up measurement, and a second privacy line (card J-14).

### C. No history

Rejected: the reader who copies, loses it and copies again is the case ADR-0065 left open, and a
history of Marxy's own copies costs no privacy that a copy did not already cost.

## Decision (proposed)

1. **Option A.** History holds what Marxy itself copied, recorded where it writes, and what the
   reader adds with *Keep the clipboard*. Nothing watches the pasteboard; ADR-0065 item 3 is unchanged.
2. **Option B is deferred, not rejected.** Its design is in this record and in card J-14 so the author
   can choose it instead, or later. It is built only after a ruling that amends ADR-0065 item 3.
3. **One setting, three values,** in `config.toml`, as ADR-0044 shapes a privacy setting:
   `clipboard_history = "off" | "session" | "keep"`, default **`session`**.
   - `off`: nothing is recorded; the view says history is off.
   - `session`: history lives in memory and is gone when Marxy quits. Nothing is written to disk.
   - `keep`: history is kept between launches as plain files (item 5).
4. **Retention.** At most `clipboard_keep_items` unpinned items (default 500; 200 in `session`) and,
   in `keep`, none older than `clipboard_keep_days` (default 30; `0` means no age limit). Pinned items
   never expire and do not count. One item is at most 1 MB of text; the store as a whole at most
   32 MB, the oldest unpinned items going first. Copying the same text as the newest item moves it to
   the top instead of adding one. Retention runs at launch and after each add.
5. **Storage is plain files in Marxy's data directory, never SQLite** (commitment 3;
   `docs/design/11-config-and-storage.md` gives the data directory):

   ```
   <data>/clipboard/history.json      {"version": 1, "items": [ …metadata… ]}
   <data>/clipboard/items/<id>.txt    the plain text, exactly as written, UTF-8
   <data>/clipboard/items/<id>.html   the HTML representation, when the copy had one
   ```

   An item's metadata is `id` (time-sortable, `20261010T142233-3f9a`), `at`, `bytes`, `format` (the
   Copy as target id), `source` (`{path, start, end, line}` or `null`), `origin` (`"copy"`,
   `"workbench"` or `"kept"`), `pinned` and `reps`. Nothing else: no app usage counts, no model,
   session or tool names, no hashes tied to a person. Writes are atomic (`writeFileAtomic`), item
   files before the index. At launch, item files the index does not name are deleted and index
   entries whose files are missing are dropped. An index that does not parse is renamed
   `history.json.bad-<timestamp>` and history starts empty, with one notice, as every data file does.
6. **Deleting is deleting.** Delete, *Clear unpinned* and *Clear everything* remove the files at
   once. Undo, for 10 seconds, holds the removed items in memory and writes them back. Moving from
   `keep` to `session` or `off` asks once and deletes `clipboard/`.
7. **Secrets.** `clipboard_skip_secrets = true` by default. Before an item is recorded, its text is
   matched in memory against a fixed list in core: AWS access keys, GitHub and GitLab tokens, Slack
   tokens, Stripe live keys, OpenAI-style and Anthropic-style API keys, PEM private-key blocks, and
   JSON Web Tokens. A match is not recorded and leaves a *not recorded* row.
8. **The Privacy page names the whole action.** The sentence, shown whenever
   `clipboard_history` is not `off`, is the constant `CLIPBOARD_HISTORY_PRIVACY_LINE`, verbatim:
   "Marxy keeps what you copy in Marxy on this Mac, in memory until you quit or, if you choose to keep
   it, as plain files in Marxy's data folder. It reads what other apps copy only when you paste or ask
   it to, and sends nothing anywhere."
   Under option B, while watching is on, the page adds `CLIPBOARD_WATCH_PRIVACY_LINE`, verbatim:
   "While Marxy is running it also records what you copy in other apps, except concealed, transient
   and auto-generated items, apps you excluded and text that looks like a secret; it keeps them as
   above and sends nothing anywhere."
9. **Nothing leaves the machine,** and no part of history is a network feature. No sync, no export
   other than the reader copying an item, no telemetry in any form.

## Consequences

- The reader gets back anything they copied in Marxy this session, with no setting touched; a reader
  who wants it across launches chooses `keep` and sees where the files are.
- `docs/design/11-config-and-storage.md` gains the `clipboard/` rows in *Data files*, the four keys,
  and an exception under *What is never stored*: copied text, when the reader chose `keep` (card J-08).
- The Clipboard view (`studio/history.md`) shows only Marxy's copies and kept items; its search is a
  substring scan over at most 32 MB in memory, with no index (recorded against the palette's 16 ms
  budget, not gated, ADR-0032).
- The ring (`⌥⌘V` over other apps) is worth much less with only Marxy's copies, which is one more
  reason it waits (`studio/ring.md`).
- FileVault is the only encryption at rest. A reader who copies client data and wants no disk trace
  keeps `session`, the default.

## Rejected

- **SQLite with FTS5** (the mockup). Commitment 3: Marxy's state is plain files a reader can read,
  move and delete.
- **History on by default with watching** (the mockup). It records every app's copies with no reader
  action, against ADR-0065 item 3 and commitment 2.
- **`keep` as the default.** It writes copied document text to disk with no reader choice; `session`
  gives the same recovery inside one sitting.
- **Encryption with a Keychain key** (the mockup's open question 6). A new privileged capability for a
  threat `session` already answers.
- **Showing a concealed item masked,** as nspasteboard.org allows. Marxy keeps nothing of it.

## How we would know this was wrong

1. Readers ask for what they copied in other apps, or press *Keep the clipboard* often: the evidence
   for option B (or for the ring with B).
2. Readers turn history `off`: `session` was not felt as private, or the view was noise.
3. A secret reaches `clipboard/items/`: the pattern list missed a common shape, and grows by test.
4. A reader is surprised to find copied text on disk: the `keep` choice is not visible enough where it
   acts.
